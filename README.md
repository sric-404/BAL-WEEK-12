# Diabetes Health Analysis Dashboard

## Objective
To analyze the diabetes healthcare dataset using Tableau Public and identify meaningful patterns and relationships associated with diabetes outcomes.

## Visualizations
1. Diabetes Status Distribution
2. Average Glucose by Diabetes Status
3. BMI vs Glucose Scatter Plot
4. Diabetes Cases by Age Group

## Interactive Filters
- Diabetes Status
- Age

## Key Findings

1. **Diabetes Distribution:** Out of 768 records, **268 patients were diabetic** and **500 were non-diabetic**, meaning approximately **34.9%** of the records were classified as diabetic.

2. **Glucose and Diabetes:** Diabetic patients had an average glucose level of **141.26**, compared with **109.98** for non-diabetic patients. The diabetic group therefore had an average glucose level approximately **31.28 points higher**.

3. **Age, BMI, and Diabetes:** The visualizations show that diabetes cases occur across different age and BMI levels. The scatter plot also shows a greater concentration of diabetic observations at higher glucose levels, indicating that glucose is an important factor associated with diabetes outcomes in this dataset.

## Suggestions / Recommendations

1. **Focus on glucose monitoring:** Since the diabetic group has substantially higher average glucose levels, regular glucose monitoring can help identify individuals who may require further medical assessment.

2. **Use multiple health indicators:** Glucose should be considered together with factors such as **BMI and age** when assessing diabetes risk rather than relying on a single measure.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows a clear relationship between **higher glucose levels and diabetes outcomes**. Although most records in the dataset are non-diabetic, approximately one-third are diabetic. Age and BMI provide additional context, while glucose shows the clearest difference between the two groups. Overall, monitoring multiple health indicators can support better identification of patterns associated with diabetes.
